Ascend is a local-first workout tracker. The current release has no account or cloud-sync feature. This policy explains what stays on your device, the Apple Health data Ascend can read with your permission, and the limited crash diagnostics that can leave your device. For rules on using the app itself, including exercise and injury disclaimers, see the terms of use.

Data stored on your device

Your workout plans, workout sessions, completed sets, weights, reps, exercise history, goals, settings, and profile preferences are stored locally. Ascend does not upload this content to us or to an account service. Limited crash diagnostics are the only exception, as described below.

If you use the companion Apple Watch app, current workout state can pass directly between your paired iPhone and Apple Watch through Apple's device-connectivity service. We do not receive it. Deleting Ascend removes its locally stored app data, subject to operating-system backup behavior.

Health and fitness data

On iOS, Ascend requests read access to exactly six Apple Health data types:

  • Step count
  • Heart rate
  • Active energy burned (active calories)
  • Walking and running distance
  • Cycling distance
  • Swimming distance

Access is off by default. Ascend asks only after you enable health metrics and Apple displays its permission prompt. Steps, heart rate, and active calories appear in activity summaries. Distance readings are used only to suggest a total after a matching walking, running, cycling, or swimming session; you can accept, replace, or ignore that suggestion.

Apple Health readings remain on your devices. Ascend reads them on demand and does not upload them to us, Sentry, or any analytics provider. If you accept a suggested step or distance total, that value becomes part of your locally stored workout record. Ascend requests no write access and writes nothing to Apple Health.

You can revoke access at any time in the iPhone Health app or in iOS Settings, or by turning health metrics off inside Ascend.

Crash diagnostics

Ascend uses Sentry to find JavaScript runtime crashes and serious data-loss failures. Native fatal-crash capture is disabled because those reports cannot pass through Ascend's JavaScript privacy filter. Sharing is off by default and controlled by More → Privacy → Share anonymous usage data. Turning that switch off stops new reports.

  • A report can contain error type, code stack frames, whether the error was handled, app version and build, operating-system version, device model and architecture, and fixed diagnostic tags such as the affected screen or storage operation.

Before a Sentry error event is sent, Ascend rebuilds it from an allowlist. Error messages, breadcrumbs, console output, request URLs and bodies, headers, cookies, account or user context, screenshots, view hierarchies, local variables, attachments, and arbitrary extra fields are removed. Workout content, sets, reps, weights, exercise names, notes, goals, profile values, and Apple Health readings are not allowed into the transmitted event.

Ascend does not currently send product-analytics events to PostHog or another product-analytics service. There is no advertising identifier, cross-app tracking, session replay, or screen recording. We will update this policy before enabling any additional analytics collection.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell or rent your data.
  • We do not share your data with advertisers, and there is no advertising in the app.
  • We do not use advertising or attribution SDKs, and the diagnostics described above are not used for advertising.
  • We do not build advertising profiles or track you across other apps and websites.
  • We do not send authentication tokens, workout content, or Apple Health readings to diagnostics.

Who processes your data

Sentry processes the limited crash diagnostics described above only while the sharing switch is on. We require service providers that handle this data to protect it consistently with this policy and applicable law. Ascend does not currently send account, workout, product analytics, or Apple Health data to Supabase, PostHog, or another cloud provider.

Your choices and rights

  • Turn off crash diagnostics. Go to More → Privacy → Share anonymous usage data. New Sentry reports stop.
  • Revoke Apple Health access. Use the iPhone Health app or iOS Settings, or turn health metrics off inside Ascend.
  • Delete local data. Remove Ascend from your device. Data in an operating-system device backup remains subject to that provider's backup controls.
  • Ask about diagnostic data. Contact us using the details below. Diagnostic reports are not tied to an Ascend account, so we may be unable to identify a particular report as yours.

Depending on where you live, you may have additional rights over your personal data, such as access, correction, deletion, and portability. Contact us to exercise them.

Security

Workout and Apple Health data is processed on your devices. Crash diagnostics are transmitted to Sentry over encrypted connections (HTTPS/TLS), after Ascend's allowlist removes the fields described above. No storage or transmission method is 100% secure, so absolute security cannot be guaranteed.

Data retention

Local workout and preference data remains until you delete it in Ascend, reset the app, or remove the app, subject to your operating system's backup behavior. Sentry crash diagnostics are retained only for the retention period configured for our Sentry project and are then deleted. You may contact us to request earlier deletion, although reports that contain no account identifier may not be identifiable as yours.

Children

Ascend is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their personal data. If you believe a child's information reached our crash diagnostics, contact us.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes materially, the updated version will be published here with a new date, and the change will be noted in the app release notes.
